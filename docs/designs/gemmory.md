# Design: Gemmory, an Android diamond-painting game built around your own photos

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-10-12
Branch: none (new project, no repo yet)
Repo: none yet (to be created under BrewingBytes)
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Startup

## Problem Statement
People who play diamond-painting / color-by-number games are limited to stock artwork. The founder wants a Google Play (Android-first) diamond-painting game: tap or drag numbered gems onto a grid. It earns money from ads and a VIP subscription. What sets it apart: the player turns **their own photo** into a diamond canvas they still recognize and can finish on a phone, then **shares** a sparkling time-lapse of it.

## Demand Evidence
- The category is proven: color-by-number and diamond apps reach tens of millions of installs, and the top 10 together get about 16-18M downloads a month (wnhub 2024 round-table).
- Founder and people they know play these apps, and the apps they use lack photo import ("Feature missing").
- *Unverified:* the 16-18M/month figure comes from a secondhand round-table summary with no primary source. Treat it as directional only (CEO review, 2026-10-12).
- **Weak spots:** no named players, and no sign that anyone would pay or switch apps for photo import.
- Status-quo answer: when they want their own photo as art, they do "Nothing, keep playing." That makes photo import a nice-to-have. It only drives installs if the share loop turns finished pictures into content other people see.

## Status Quo
Players keep using whatever stock pictures their current app offers. Photo import exists in at least one competitor: Dotterly (photo → pattern + printable PDF). It's primarily iOS (v2.10.x). A third-party aggregator also lists an Android v1.0.0 build ([mwm.ai listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/id/6446167461)); this hasn't been verified on Google Play yet. DiamondCrafts (iOS) also converts photos. Neither is a game with a share loop built around a time-lapse. The real competitor is an existing app's built-in library, which is "good enough."

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- **Primary:** people who already play diamond / color-by-number apps for 30-60 min a day to relax.
- **Secondary:** physical-kit hobbyists who want to play their own photos in the app between real kits.
- **Narrowest wedge:** convert a photo into a recognizable, finishable diamond canvas, then share a 9:16 sparkle time-lapse. If the converter output doesn't make someone say "that's my dog," nothing else matters.

## Constraints
- **Everything happens in the app. No physical kits, no fulfillment, no affiliate kit sales** (explicit founder decision).
- Android first, published on Google Play.
- Built with Kotlin Multiplatform so iOS can follow without a rewrite. The converter and game logic live in `commonMain` as pure Kotlin.
- **Google Play requirement:** a new personal developer account needs a closed test with 12+ testers for 14 days before production. This clock can't be shortened.
- **Ad constraints:**
  - No ads on the canvas while playing.
  - Interstitials only after a picture is finished, under the cap in **Ad rules** below.
  - Rewarded ads are opt-in.
  - No banner ads in the MVP. The gallery banner is deferred to v1.1 (CEO review CUT-1).
  - The audience must not target children. This avoids the Play Families policy and AdMob child-directed restrictions; declare this explicitly in the Play Console.

## Premises
1. **Core gem-placing gameplay is table stakes.** It must feel great (haptics, sound, sparkle), but it isn't what sets the app apart.
2. **What sets it apart is a recognizable own-photo canvas at a size you can finish, plus a shareable sparkle time-lapse.**
   - Sizes: Quick ~3k gems, Medium ~8k, Big ~15k.
   - Converter quality, especially faces, is risk #1 and gets tested first.
3. **Money comes from ads plus a VIP subscription.**
   - AdMob rewarded and interstitial ads; interstitials only after a picture is finished.
   - Photo conversion is itself a monetization lever: 1 free photo canvas a day, extra ones via a rewarded ad or VIP.
   - VIP removes ads and unlocks everything.
4. **Android first via KMP.** Recruiting the 12 closed-test testers starts in week one.
5. **Audience:** existing app players, plus kit hobbyists playing their own photos in the app.

## Cross-Model Perspective
An independent cold read by a Claude subagent (Codex was unavailable because there's no git repo yet).
- **Steelman:** a photo-first diamond studio that people actually play, rather than another stock-picture game.
- **Key insight:** Q2's "Nothing, keep playing" shows there's no proven pain. Build the converter first and put the results in front of real players before writing gameplay code.
- **Premise it challenged:** true kit sizes and recognizable faces can't both be playable. A 40x50 cm kit is about 32k gems, far too many to tap through on a phone. Small grids blur faces.
  - **Resolved:** physical kits are out of scope, so the in-app sizes only need to be playable. Faces still need to stay recognizable at Quick, Medium and Big.
- **Prototype it suggested:** a 48-hour prototype.
  - Pipeline: CIELAB color conversion, k-means color reduction, merging tiny speckled regions, a single Compose Canvas with pan/zoom (not one composable per cell), a number overlay that appears when zoomed in, and a before/after share image.
  - It would skip ads and the content library.
- **Rejected:** its suggestion of a "$39 kit" fake-door button. The founder rejected physical kits.

## Approaches Considered
### Approach A: Photo Diamond MVP
- **What it includes:**
  - Android Compose app with KMP modules.
  - Photo → grid converter.
  - Single-Canvas gem renderer.
  - About 30 pictures bundled in the app.
  - Progress saved on the phone.
  - 9:16 sparkle time-lapse shared through the share sheet.
  - AdMob (rewarded + interstitial after finishing a picture).
  - Play Billing VIP / remove ads.
- **Effort and risk:** L (human ~8-10 weeks / CC ~2 weeks). Risk: Med. Widened at the CEO review (R3-12) to cover ML Kit + faceMinSize, backup exclusion, analytics, locked content, allowance logic, 3 billing products and CI upload. See **Timeline**.
### Approach B: Full live-ops game
- **Adds to A:** pictures delivered from a server (weekly packs, a daily free picture, events), Compose Multiplatform iOS UI, mediation, cloud save, streaks.
- **Effort and risk:** XL (human ~4-6 months / CC ~2-3 weeks). Risk: Med-High.
- **Why not now:** months before any player data.
### Approach C: Converter-first spike, then A
- **Week 1:** converter + preview + share output only, tested on about 20 real photos. Results get shown to players and posted in communities.
- **Then:** build A around the proven converter.

## Recommended Approach
**C then A** (chosen by the founder). The converter is the single biggest risk, and a converter spike costs only days. The before/after posts double as recruitment for the 12 required Play testers. B gets built step by step on top of A once there's real retention and ad-revenue data.

**Spike (C) scope:**
- KMP `commonMain` converter module in pure Kotlin:
  - Downscale.
  - Optional contrast and saturation boost.
  - CIELAB color conversion.
  - k-means color reduction with a palette cap per size (Quick ~16, Medium ~24, Big ~36 colors).
  - Dithering off by default.
  - Merge small regions.
  - Number the color slots.
- Minimal Android screen: pick photo → crop → choose size → render the gem preview → export a PNG before/after.

**Spike gate (go / iterate / kill):**
- **GO to A** when Medium passes for faces and the "I'd play this" rating passes. The exact pass rule is in Success Criteria. A Quick-only face miss still goes straight to GO, and the face size rule is set to `faceMinSize` = Medium.
- **ITERATE:** if Medium misses for faces or the rating misses, up to 2 more converter iterations, at most 5 working days in total. Examples: face-aware cropping, a bigger palette, edge-preserving smoothing, a separate skin-tone palette.
- **RE-SCOPE** if it still misses after that:
  - (A Quick-only face miss never reaches re-scope; see GO above.)
  - If Medium also fails on faces, run both recognition tests at **Big** using the same pass rule (Medium thresholds). If Big passes, offer face photo canvases only at Big; pets and landscapes stay at all sizes. If Big fails, drop faces from photo canvases: when a face is detected, the app says "Faces aren't supported yet" and still allows pets and landscapes.
  - If pets and landscapes also fail, photo import stops being the headline feature. The app ships as a polished bundled-picture game where sharing the time-lapse is the main differentiator, and the founder re-runs office hours before building further.

**Spike result (2026-10-13): GO.** The founder ran the spike and reported that all tests passed. Quick wasn't tested for faces (T6), so `faceMinSize` = Medium (the default row in the table below). The converter defaults at GO are saturation 1.0, no speck merging, lightness weight 2.5 and sharpen 0.65; they come from a face tuning pass on 11 portraits. The 27 converter goldens (9 specs × face/pet/landscape) are frozen in `shared/src/jvmTest/goldens`, and `ConverterGoldenTest` runs in CI as part of `:shared:allTests` (D19).

**Face detection ships in the MVP no matter what the gate decides.**
- Uses ML Kit's bundled on-device face detection, so no photo leaves the phone and photos remain "not collected." ML Kit still sends diagnostic and usage data (device info, app info, performance metrics, API configuration) to Google. Review Google's ML Kit data-disclosure guidance before Play submission and declare that diagnostics data in the Data safety form if required (R3-6).
- The gate outcome only changes which size rule applies to faces. It's a single config value: `faceMinSize` = Quick | Medium | Big | Disabled.
- Default rule (`faceMinSize` = Medium): when the cropped photo contains a face, the size picker shows "Faces look best at Medium or Big", pre-selects Medium and disables Quick, as in the table below (founder, 2026-10-13, during #13: the table and design-system.md win over an earlier "Quick stays selectable" line).
- Sizes below `faceMinSize` are always disabled for face photos.
- **Mixed photos (R3-4):** if ML Kit detects any face in the cropped photo, the face rule applies to the whole photo, including photos with a pet or landscape too.
- **Gate outcome → picker behavior (R3-3):**

| Gate outcome | `faceMinSize` | Hint on face photos | Pre-selected | Disabled for face photos |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quick AND Medium pass for faces (unreachable after D13: Quick isn't tested for faces; needs a separate fresh-circle Quick test) | Quick | none | Medium (default for all photos) | none |
| Medium passes, Quick fails (default expectation) | Medium | "Faces look best at Medium or Big" | Medium | Quick |
| Only Big passes (re-scope) | Big | "Faces need Big" | Big | Quick, Medium |
| Big also fails (re-scope) | Disabled | "Faces aren't supported yet" | n/a (Start blocked) | all |

- Non-face photos (no face detected) always offer all three sizes and pre-select Medium.

**User photos and privacy (decided):**
- Conversion runs entirely on the phone. No photo or grid is ever uploaded, and the app has no backend for user content.
- Photos are picked through the Android Photo Picker, so no broad media permission is needed.
- Stored per photo canvas, in app-private storage:
  - the cropped, downscaled source (max 1024 px, needed for the before/after share and the time-lapse intro)
  - the generated grid
  - progress
- The full-resolution original is never copied.
- Deleting a photo canvas from the gallery (long-press → Delete) removes all three. Uninstalling removes everything.
- **Cloud backup and device transfer are excluded:**
  - `dataExtractionRules` (Android 12+) and `fullBackupContent` (older versions) exclude the photo-canvas directory and the allowance counter from both cloud backup and device-to-device transfer.
  - Nothing user-photo-derived ever reaches Google Drive, and a reinstall really does start clean.
  - Bundled-picture progress may be backed up, since it contains no user content.
- Data safety: photos are "not collected" (processed on the phone, never transmitted). The analytics and ad identifiers below are declared.

**Locked pictures (#32, founder 2026-10-16):**
- Of the 49 bundled pictures after the starter, 6 are free: Lemons on Blue Tiles and Hot Air Balloon (Quick), Cherries and Milk Jug and Tuscan Hills (Medium), Golden Puppy and Ginger Cat (Big). The other 42 are locked, including all 21 CC0/public-domain ones (CONTENT-1). This replaces the earlier ~20 free / ~10 locked default.
- The free set is one list, `BundledPictures.FREE`. Every other picture, including any added later, is locked. Editing that list changes no grid or CRC. `BundledPicturesTest` checks that every id exists, that the starter is never locked, and that each size keeps at least 2 free pictures.
- **Gallery order (#58, founder 2026-10-16):** the starter, then every picture the player can open (free, unlocked by a video or grace, or started), then every locked one, each group Quick, Medium, Big in catalog order. A free player's first 7 tiles are the starter and the 6 free pictures. VIP has nothing locked, so it sees the plain #31 ramp. "Can open" is the rule a tap uses (`isLocked`). The order is set when the Gallery shows, so a picture unlocked by a video moves up on the next visit. `GalleryOrderTest` covers Free, a video unlock, a started locked picture and VIP.
- A locked picture opens after one rewarded video (permanent), today's grace unlock on an online no-fill, or VIP. A picture with at least one gem placed stays open whatever the entitlement (eng E4c). When VIP lapses, pictures unlocked by a video or started stay open and the rest lock again. A "Start over" empties a picture, so after a lapse it locks again unless a video unlocked it.
- **Today's picture (#34, founder 2026-10-17):** one locked picture a day, neither unlocked nor started, picked on the phone (`TodaysPicture`, commonMain: date-seeded over the candidates in catalog order, remembered for the local day so starting it doesn't move it, avoiding yesterday's pick while another is left). Free to start that day with no video; its first gem writes a permanent unlock, like a video's. Not started by local midnight, it locks again. VIP gets "Today's pick" over the pictures not started, with nothing to unlock. With 42 locked pictures, a daily player meets a new one each day for weeks; when nothing is left to pick, no card shows. Shown as a card above the Pictures grid (design system, Today's picture). A moved clock or time zone can show another picture, and a data wipe re-picks the day: the same accepted risk as the allowance. It doesn't touch the ad rules (no ad to start it, nothing on the canvas) or the streak.

**More colors (#29, founder 2026-10-15 and 2026-10-18):** a color-count choice for photo canvases.
- **Choice:** Fewer / Standard / More on Preview (design system, Preview + Start). Caps (`CanvasSize.cap`, commonMain): Quick 12 / 16 / 24, Medium 16 / 24 / 40, Big 24 / 36 / 60. Standard is the cap from before #29 (`paletteCap`), which the size picker, bundled pictures and every app before versionCode 12 use. All stay ≤ 255, so the GMRY grid (u16 palette, u8 cells) and the save format don't change; a canvas keeps the palette it was made with.
- **Tiers:** Fewer and Standard are free; More is VIP, or one rewarded video for that photo draft (`reward_type = colors`, with its own daily grace on an online no-fill). A canvas video watched on that Preview also unlocks More, so a free player never watches two videos for one canvas (`PhotoFlowRules.previewPrimary`).
- **Converter:** More converts at its cap, then merges palette colors closer than 5 in the converter's clustering space (Lab with L ×2.5) into their cell-weighted mean (`ConverterOptions.minColorDistance`), so two near-identical shades don't become two numbers. Tuned at Big 60 on the fixtures (face 60, pet 47, landscape 52 colors; closest plain ΔE76 pair ≥ 2.1); a plain-Lab rule flattened dark fur, and 6 began to band skies. Fewer and Standard don't merge, so their output and the converter goldens are unchanged. Big at 60 converts in ~20-50 ms on the JVM.
- **Bundled pictures:** unchanged (founder 2026-10-18, option A). Re-freezing a shipped picture changes its grid CRC, so every started save would be refused ("Can't open"); richer pictures arrive as remote pictures instead (`-Pcolors=more`, `minAppVersion` ≥ 12; README).
- **Renderer:** up to 60 troughs scroll in the tray; numbers stay at two digits on 16dp cells. The sprite atlas grows with the palette (at 48dp cells and 3× density ~15 MB for 60 colors, ~9 MB for 36).

**Remote pictures (#43, founder 2026-10-17):** new pictures without a Play release.
- **Hosting:** `pictures/v1/` on `main` of this (public) repo, served from `raw.githubusercontent.com/BrewingBytes/Gemmory/main/pictures/v1/` (founder: same repo, a folder, over a separate content repo). It holds `catalog.json`, `catalog.json.sig` and `grids/<id>.gmrg`. Publishing is a PR like any change: `main` is protected, so a picture goes out once the `build` check passes and it's merged. The URL is a build config field (`REMOTE_PICTURES_URL`; debug builds take `-Pgemmory.remotePicturesUrl=`).
- **Data, never code:** only finished GMRY grids made by the same freeze as bundled pictures (`./gradlew :shared:publishRemotePicture`, README). Grids are immutable (saves bind to the grid CRC, eng D11): a fix is a new id, the old one withdrawn.
- **Catalog v1** (`RemoteCatalogs`, commonMain): `version`, `issuedAt` (epoch seconds; a catalog older than the one accepted is refused, so an old signed one can't be replayed), and per picture `id`, `title`, `ratio`, `size`, `width`, `height` (must be the picker's spec for that size and ratio), `colors` (up to the size's More cap, #29; above Standard only with `minAppVersion` ≥ 12, since an older app refuses the whole catalog over such an entry), `grid` (must be `grids/<id>.gmrg`), `sha256`, `crc32`, `locked`, `minAppVersion` (a versionCode; pictures for a newer app are skipped unread), `addedAt`, optional `availableFrom` (a local date, T36; only with `minAppVersion` ≥ 13 = `DROPS_MIN_APP`, since an older app would show it at once), and optional `rewardOf` (a collection id, T37; only with `minAppVersion` ≥ 15 = `REWARDS_MIN_APP`). An optional top-level `collections` list follows the pictures (T37, below). A malformed catalog, an id that a bundled picture uses or one listed twice refuses the whole catalog.
- **Signing:** Ed25519 over the exact catalog bytes (`catalog.json.sig`, base64). The app holds a list of public keys (`RemoteKeys.PRODUCTION`, one now) and verifies in pure Kotlin (minSdk 29 has no platform Ed25519). The private key is `~/.gemmory/remote-pictures-ed25519.pem` on the founder's machine (path in `local.properties` as `remotePictures.signingKey`), with a copy in the founder's password manager, never in a repo. Every grid is checked for its SHA-256, GMRY file, CRC and shape before it's stored, and again when read.
- **Fetch:** when the Gallery shows, if the last success is 24 h old and the last attempt 1 h old (`RemoteSchedule`), in the background on the app scope with `HttpURLConnection` (no new dependency) and If-None-Match. Cancelled when a canvas opens (nothing downloads during play) and retried at the next Gallery visit. Offline, the cached catalog and grids keep working. No WorkManager.
- **Storage and backup:** `remote/` (catalog, signature, the "GMRR" state with the ETag, check times and "New" bookkeeping, and `grids/`) is excluded from backup and device transfer: it can be downloaded again. On first open a grid is copied into `canvases/remote/<id>/` with the catalog title in its record, and progress is saved exactly like a bundled picture's; that directory is backed up (16 KB at most a grid), so a restored save opens even after its picture left the catalog.
- **Withdraw:** a picture the catalog no longer lists disappears from the gallery and its downloaded grid is deleted, unless the player has started it (a gem placed, the E4c started rule); a started one keeps playing from its own copy.
- **Gallery (founder picks from the mockups, `~/.gstack/projects/Gemmory/designs/t27-remote-pictures/`):** a remote picture joins its size's run in the #58 order, after the bundled pictures of that size, oldest published first, in the open or locked group like any picture. A "New" label marks one that arrived in the last 7 days until it's opened; nothing is new on a phone's first sync. New pictures appear when the check that brought them finishes (T36) or on the next Gallery visit.
- **Locks and the rest:** `locked` is the catalog's flag, with the same `isLocked` rule: a video, an unlock token (#38) or VIP opens it, and it can be Today's picture (#34), which prefers a new remote picture when one is eligible.
- **Telemetry:** `canvas_started` / `canvas_finished` with `source = remote`. Catalog and grid refusals, HTTP errors and write failures are Crashlytics non-fatals (`RemotePictureException`, codes only); being offline isn't reported. No event for a catalog check (founder: it would fire every day for everyone and say little that remote starts don't).
- **Privacy:** GitHub (Microsoft) receives the IP address and user agent of each download, named in the privacy policy; no new Data safety type (docs/play-compliance.md).
- **Dated drops (T36, #77; CEO C2, C4, eng D4, D9, D12):** a picture with `availableFrom` is a drop for that local date (one a day is the plan, published weeks ahead).
  - **Hidden means hidden:** the check downloads and verifies its grid at once, then `RemotePictures.listed()` and `grid(id)`, which every caller goes through (summaries, opening a canvas, `pictureRef`, `openableRef` for ids from outside the gallery), leave it out until its local date. A crafted or stale id for tomorrow's drop opens nothing and leaves the player on the gallery. A drop not stored and verified by its date stays hidden until it is.
  - **New and Today's picture:** its "New" label counts from `availableFrom`, not the day it arrived (on a phone's first sync, a drop whose date already passed counts as seen before the player). On its date it's preferred as Today's picture over every other picture, and the Today card shows it with "New today" even if the day's pick was made earlier. The days after, it's never preferred as a New picture, so it isn't free again through the card.
  - **Free on its date only (D4, D9):** a free player starts it without a video on its local date, beside the day's pick, which stays free and doesn't move; its first gem unlocks it for good (the Today's picture path, decided at open). Arriving on date+1 or later it follows its catalog `locked` flag.
  - **Tomorrow line:** when tomorrow's drop is stored and verified, the Today card (or a card of its own) ends with its frost and "A new picture opens at midnight" (design system). A tap says "Opens tomorrow". Drops open at local midnight: the gallery reloads then while it shows, and after a check that brings something new.
  - **Summaries (D12):** a remote picture never opened is summarized from its catalog entry; its grid is read only to draw its thumbnail when the tile shows, or the Today card. `GalleryLoadTimingTest` fails if 365 remote pictures make `summaries()` more than twice as slow as the bundled ones alone.
  - **Accepted risks:** a moved clock or time zone shows a drop early or late; the art of a published drop is public in the repo before its date.
- **Collections (T37, #78; eng D7, CEO C6, plan-design-review D3-D7, D25):** the catalog's optional top-level `collections` list, `[{"id", "title", "members": [picture ids], optional "event": true}]`, in catalog order (the last listed is the newest). Members may be remote or bundled ids, and need not be listed (a newer app's picture, or a withdrawn one); collection ids share one namespace with events, apart from picture ids (a clash refuses the catalog). Old apps ignore the key, so collections never raise a picture's `minAppVersion`.
  - **Keepsake:** a picture with `rewardOf` = a collection id, only with `minAppVersion` ≥ 15 = `REWARDS_MIN_APP` (an older app would show it at once). It's downloaded ahead and hidden by `listed()` and `grid()` until its id is in the unlocks.
  - **Progress:** a member counts once finished, whatever its date (pictures finished before T35 too); one in the catalog but not on the gallery yet is "coming" and blocks completion; one neither in the catalog nor started leaves the collection and its size; a started one stays. A phone that never had a verified catalog has no collections (the cached one counts offline).

Encoder fallback chain (E1):
```
targets = [1080x1920@30, 720x1280@30]
for t in targets:
   probe areSizeAndRateSupported + alignment ── no ──► next t
   configure ── CodecException/ISE ──► delete partial, non-fatal ──► next t
   render frames (incremental bitmap → EGL surface + eglPresentationTimeANDROID; #4) → MediaMuxer
       ── error / cancel ──► delete partial (cancel: stop, no fallback)
   success ──► rename into cache if canvas still exists (E4d) ──► share sheet + utm text (D14)
all failed ──► before/after PNG + "Video isn't supported on this phone…" ──► share sheet
```
- **Implementation note (#4, 2026-10-14):** frames go to the encoder's input surface through EGL, not `lockHardwareCanvas`. On the gemmory_pixel emulator `lockHardwareCanvas` dropped 1-6 of 450 frames (its producer runs at swap interval 0, so a frame posted before the encoder took the last one replaces it) and stamped frames with wall-clock time. An EGL window surface blocks instead, and `eglPresentationTimeANDROID` gives each frame its FramePlan time. The incremental canvas bitmap (finding 12) is unchanged; it is uploaded as a texture on frames that placed gems. Before each video target the chain checks free space against that target's need (bitrate × 15 s × 1.5): 1080 is skipped silently when only 720 fits, and when even 720 doesn't fit it's out of space. A real ENOSPC stops the chain at once (no smaller try, no picture), and a partial file that vanishes (the system clears cacheDir under storage pressure) counts as out of space.
Save load path (E2, D3, D4, D11):
```
read file ─► magic ok? ─► version ≤ current? ─► CRC ok? ─► migrate(v→current) ─► grid CRC matches? ─► indices valid (no dup / in range)?
   any "no" ─► refuse, keep last good file, Crashlytics non-fatal, user sees last good progress
```

### R-X1: Re-share after Start over / Play again (finding 1)
Finding: 1, P2, 8/10, gemmory.md:740, reviewer /plan-eng-review

Question D1:
D1 — How do we stop a replayed picture from sharing its old video?
Project/branch/task: BrewingBytes/Gemmory main, eng re-check of design D3 vs eng D2.
ELI10: Finished videos are cached so re-sharing is instant. The cache is named by canvas, number of gems placed, and watermark. A finished picture always has the same gem count, so if someone taps "Play again" and finishes it a second time, Share would hand them the first video. We can either clear that canvas's cached video whenever it restarts, or put a "playthrough number" into the cache name.
Stakes if we pick wrong: a player shares a video that doesn't match what they just played. It's subtle and looks like a bug.
Recommendation: B because Start over already has to cancel encodes and wipe progress, so wiping the cached video in the same step reuses the E4d path and keeps the key unchanged.
Note: options differ in kind, not coverage — no completeness score.
Header: Video cache
Options:
A) Playthrough id in key
Mint a random playthrough id at canvas start and on Start over, keep it in the view file, and add it to the cache key. Old MP4s age out with cacheDir. Human ~1h / CC ~10min. A third key field, plus stale files until the OS clears cache.
B) Clear on restart (recommended)
Start over, Play again and Delete call EncodeManager.cancel(canvasId), then delete that canvas's cached MP4s (the E4d path). The key is unchanged. Human ~30min / CC ~5min. One test: restart, finish, Share encodes fresh.
